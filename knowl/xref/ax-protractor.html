Axiom 11.4.2. Protractor Axiom.

The number of degrees in an angle (also known as its degree-measure) is always a number between \(0^\circ\) and \(360^\circ\text{.}\) The number of degrees of an ordinary angle is less than \(180^\circ\text{.}\) It has these properties:
🔗
  1. A straight angle has \(180^\circ\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  2. Given a ray \([AB\text{,}\) and a number \(d\) between \(0\) and \(180\text{,}\) there is exactly one ray from \(A\) on each side of the line \(AB\) that makes an (ordinary) angle having \(d\) degrees with the ray \([AB\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
  3. If \(D\) is a point inside an angle \(\angle BAC\text{,}\) then
    \begin{equation*} |\angle BAC| = |\angle BAD| + |\angle DAC|. \end{equation*}
    🔗
    🔗
Null angles are assigned \(0^\circ\text{,}\) full angles \(360^\circ\text{,}\) and reflex angles have more than \(180^\circ\text{.}\) If \(A\text{,}\) \(B\text{,}\) and \(C\) are noncollinear points, then the reflex angle outside the angle \(\angle BAC\) measures \(360^\circ - |\angle BAC|\text{.}\)
